WHO Essential Medicines — Key Drug Interactions Reference
Source: WHO Model List of Essential Medicines (22nd Edition, 2021) + WHO Drug Information

SECTION 1 — CARDIOVASCULAR DRUG INTERACTIONS
1.1 ACE Inhibitors (Enalapril, Ramipril, Lisinopril)
- ACE Inhibitor + ARB (e.g., Losartan): AVOID COMBINATION. Dual blockade increases risk of hypotension, hyperkalemia, and acute kidney injury without additional cardiovascular benefit (ONTARGET trial).
- ACE Inhibitor + Potassium-sparing diuretic (Spironolactone): HIGH RISK of hyperkalemia; monitor potassium closely if combined; especially dangerous in CKD.
- ACE Inhibitor + NSAIDs (Ibuprofen, Diclofenac): Reduces antihypertensive effect; increases risk of AKI. Avoid combination where possible.
- ACE Inhibitor + Aliskiren: Contraindicated in diabetics and CKD (increased risk of hypotension, renal impairment, hyperkalemia).

1.2 Beta-Blockers (Atenolol, Metoprolol, Carvedilol)
- Beta-Blocker + Verapamil or Diltiazem (non-dihydropyridine CCBs): RISK of severe bradycardia and heart block. Avoid IV combinations. Oral combination requires close monitoring.
- Beta-Blocker + Insulin or Sulfonylureas: Beta-blockers may mask hypoglycemia symptoms (tachycardia, tremor) — only sweating remains as warning sign.
- Beta-Blocker + Clonidine: Abrupt clonidine withdrawal with concurrent beta-blockade causes severe rebound hypertension. Taper clonidine slowly.

1.3 Statins (Atorvastatin, Rosuvastatin, Simvastatin)
- Simvastatin + Amlodipine: Limit Simvastatin dose to 20mg/day; combination increases risk of myopathy.
- Simvastatin + Erythromycin or Clarithromycin: HIGH RISK of myopathy and rhabdomyolysis. Switch to Rosuvastatin or Pravastatin for antibiotic course.
- Statin + Gemfibrozil: Significantly increased risk of myopathy. Use Fenofibrate instead if fibrate needed.
- Statin + Warfarin: Statins enhance anticoagulant effect of Warfarin; monitor INR closely when starting or changing statin.

SECTION 2 — ANTIBIOTIC DRUG INTERACTIONS
2.1 Fluoroquinolones (Ciprofloxacin, Levofloxacin, Ofloxacin)
- Fluoroquinolone + Antacids containing Aluminum/Magnesium: Reduces fluoroquinolone absorption by up to 90%. Take antacid 2 hours before or 6 hours after fluoroquinolone.
- Fluoroquinolone + Warfarin: Significantly potentiates Warfarin anticoagulation; monitor INR closely.
- Fluoroquinolone + NSAIDs: Increases risk of CNS side effects and seizures.
- Fluoroquinolone + Metformin: Can cause dysglycemia (both hypoglycemia and hyperglycemia) in diabetics; monitor blood glucose.

2.2 Macrolides (Erythromycin, Azithromycin, Clarithromycin)
- Clarithromycin + Statins (especially Simvastatin, Lovastatin): Suspend statin during Clarithromycin course; risk of rhabdomyolysis.
- Azithromycin + QT-prolonging drugs (Ondansetron, Haloperidol, Amiodarone): Additive QTc prolongation; risk of Torsades de Pointes. Avoid combination.
- Erythromycin + Carbamazepine: Significantly increases Carbamazepine levels; toxicity risk (nausea, diplopia, ataxia).

SECTION 3 — ANALGESIC AND ANTI-INFLAMMATORY INTERACTIONS
3.1 NSAIDs (Ibuprofen, Diclofenac, Naproxen, Ketorolac)
- NSAIDs + Anticoagulants (Warfarin, Heparin, DOACs): Significantly increases bleeding risk. Prefer Paracetamol (Acetaminophen) for analgesia.
- NSAIDs + Antihypertensives: Reduces antihypertensive effect of ACE inhibitors, ARBs, diuretics.
- NSAIDs + Corticosteroids: Additive risk of GI bleeding. Co-prescribe PPI (Omeprazole, Pantoprazole) if combination unavoidable.
- NSAIDs + Methotrexate: Increases Methotrexate toxicity (bone marrow suppression, renal impairment). Avoid combination.
- NSAIDs + Lithium: NSAIDs reduce renal lithium clearance, causing lithium toxicity.

3.2 Opioids (Tramadol, Codeine, Morphine)
- Tramadol + SSRIs (Fluoxetine, Sertraline): Risk of Serotonin Syndrome; symptoms include agitation, hyperthermia, clonus.
- Tramadol + MAOIs: CONTRAINDICATED; severe serotonin syndrome.
- Opioids + Benzodiazepines: Increased risk of respiratory depression and death; use lowest effective doses if combination unavoidable.

SECTION 4 — COMMON POLYPHARMACY SCENARIOS IN INDIA
- Elderly patients on ≥5 medications are at HIGH RISK for adverse drug events.
- Most common dangerous combination in Indian primary care: ACE inhibitor + NSAID + Diuretic ("Triple Whammy") — causes acute kidney injury.
- Second most common: Warfarin + Antibiotic + NSAID — causes severe bleeding.
- Patients on TB treatment (Rifampicin) must have ALL other drug interactions reviewed — Rifampicin is a powerful enzyme inducer that reduces levels of many drugs including Warfarin, oral contraceptives, and antiretrovirals.
