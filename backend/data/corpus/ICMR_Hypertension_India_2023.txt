ICMR Consensus Guidelines on Hypertension Management in India (2023)
Source: Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR) / Indian College of Physicians

SECTION 1 — EPIDEMIOLOGY (India Context)
- Hypertension affects approximately 30% of Indian adults (2023 estimates).
- Only 45% of hypertensive Indians are diagnosed; of those diagnosed, only 15% achieve target BP.
- Indians develop hypertension at a younger age compared to Western populations.
- Salt intake in India is 2–3× the WHO recommended maximum of 5g/day.
- Salt restriction (to <5g/day) alone can reduce systolic BP by 4–5 mmHg.

SECTION 2 — DEFINITION AND CLASSIFICATION (India 2023)
- Normal BP: <120/80 mmHg
- Elevated BP (pre-hypertension): 120–129 / <80 mmHg
- Stage 1 Hypertension: 130–139 / 80–89 mmHg
- Stage 2 Hypertension: ≥140/90 mmHg
- Hypertensive Urgency: BP >180/110 without end-organ damage
- Hypertensive Emergency: BP >180/120 WITH end-organ damage (chest pain, neurological changes, AKI, pulmonary oedema)

NOTE: The ICMR (2023) recommends using the American College of Cardiology (ACC/AHA) 2017 thresholds (130/80) for high-risk Indians but acknowledges resource constraints in primary care.

SECTION 3 — FIRST-LINE TREATMENT FOR HYPERTENSION IN INDIA
Step 1 (all patients, lifestyle measures first):
- Sodium restriction to <5g NaCl/day
- Weight loss if BMI >23 kg/m²
- DASH or equivalent diet (increase fruits, vegetables, reduce saturated fat)
- Physical activity: 150 minutes moderate-intensity per week
- Reduce or stop alcohol; stop tobacco

Step 2 (pharmacological, Stage 1 or failed lifestyle):
- Low-to-moderate cardiovascular risk: ANY of:
  a) Amlodipine 5mg once daily (Calcium Channel Blocker — CCB) — preferred in elderly and Afro-Indian patients
  b) Enalapril 5mg OR Ramipril 2.5mg once daily (ACE inhibitor) — preferred in diabetics with microalbuminuria
  c) Telmisartan 40mg OR Losartan 50mg once daily (ARB) — preferred if ACE inhibitor cough develops
  d) Hydrochlorothiazide 12.5mg OR Chlorthalidone 12.5mg once daily (Thiazide diuretic) — preferred if oedema present
- Choose based on comorbidities and affordability.

Step 3 (if BP uncontrolled on monotherapy):
- Combine two agents from different classes.
- Preferred combination: CCB + ACE inhibitor/ARB (e.g., Amlodipine + Ramipril)
- Alternative: CCB + Thiazide diuretic
- AVOID: ACE inhibitor + ARB (dual blockade — increases risk without additional benefit)

Step 4 (resistant hypertension — BP uncontrolled on 3 agents including diuretic):
- Refer to physician/specialist.
- Consider Spironolactone 25mg added as 4th agent (most effective add-on in resistant hypertension).
- Rule out secondary causes: renal artery stenosis, primary aldosteronism, sleep apnoea.

SECTION 4 — SPECIFIC POPULATIONS
Hypertension in Diabetics:
- Target BP: <130/80 mmHg
- First-line: ACE inhibitor or ARB (renoprotective)
- Avoid: Thiazide as first-line in diabetics (worsens glucose control at high doses)

Hypertension in CKD:
- Target BP: <130/80 mmHg
- ACE inhibitor or ARB: First-line (proteinuria reduction)
- Avoid: High-dose thiazides if eGFR <30
- Avoid: ACE inhibitor + ARB combination

Hypertension in Pregnancy:
- SAFE: Methyldopa, Nifedipine (extended release), Labetalol
- AVOID: ACE inhibitors, ARBs, direct renin inhibitors (teratogenic)
- Target: 110–140 / 80–85 mmHg

Elderly Hypertension (>65 years):
- Target: <140/90 mmHg (not <130/80 — increases fall and syncope risk in elderly)
- Prefer once-daily formulations for adherence
- Start low, go slow with dosing
- Monitor for orthostatic hypotension

SECTION 5 — MONITORING AND FOLLOW-UP
- After initiating or changing BP medication: Check BP and renal function (creatinine, potassium) at 2–4 weeks.
- ACE inhibitor/ARB: Increase in creatinine <30% from baseline is acceptable; continue. >30% increase — stop and investigate.
- Once BP is stable: Review every 3–6 months.
- Home BP monitoring (HBPM): Preferred over office BP for diagnosis and monitoring; take two readings 1 minute apart, morning and evening, for 7 days before appointment.
- White-coat hypertension: Consider 24-hour ABPM before starting treatment in patients with borderline BP.

Safety Disclaimer:
This is a clinical reference summary. Treatment must be individualised by a qualified physician.
